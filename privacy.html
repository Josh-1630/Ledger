Privacy policy

Ledger · last updated 8 November 2026

In short: your financial data stays on your device. Ledger has no server of its own, does not track you, and never sells or shares your data. If you turn on Google backup, a copy goes to a private folder in your own Google Drive that only Ledger can open.

Who we are

Ledger is a personal money-management web app published at https://josh-1630.github.io/Ledger/ by the owner of the GitHub account Josh-1630. Its source code is public at github.com/Josh-1630/Ledger.

What Ledger stores on your device

The things you enter are saved in your browser's storage on your own device (local storage and IndexedDB):

  • Transactions: amounts, dates, categories, subcategories, accounts and notes.
  • Budgets, categories, savings goals, repeating entries and settings such as currency and theme.
  • Receipt photos you attach, reduced in size on your device before they are stored.
  • If you set a PIN: a salted SHA-256 hash of it, plus hashes of any recovery code or security answer. The PIN itself is never stored.

This data is not sent to us or to any server we run. We have no way to see it.

Google sign-in and backup (optional)

If you choose Sign in with Google, Ledger asks Google for these permissions:

PermissionWhat Ledger does with it
drive.appdata
Its own app folder in your Google Drive		Saves your Ledger backup (ledger-backup.json) and your receipt photos to the hidden app-data folder in your Drive, and reads them back when you choose Restore from Google. Ledger cannot see or change any other files in your Drive.
openid, email
Your email address		Shows which Google account your backup is in. The address is kept on your device only.
  • The sign-in token Google issues is kept only in the app's memory and expires within an hour. It is sent only to Google's own servers (accounts.google.com and www.googleapis.com).
  • Your PIN hash, recovery code and security answer are never uploaded.
  • Ledger does not read your Gmail, contacts, photos or any other Google data.

Ledger's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google is used only to provide the backup and restore feature you asked for. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not shared with anyone, and not read by people.

What Ledger does not do

  • No analytics, advertising, tracking cookies or third-party scripts, apart from Google's sign-in script, which loads only when Google backup is used.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your data with anyone.
  • No access to your bank or mobile money accounts. Ledger only knows what you type into it.

Hosting

The app's files are delivered by GitHub Pages. GitHub may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses, when your device downloads those files; see GitHub's privacy statement. Your financial data is never sent to GitHub.

Keeping, exporting and deleting your data

  • Export: More, then Back up (file) or Export CSV, saves a copy you control.
  • Delete on your device: More, then Erase everything on this device. Clearing the browser's site data or uninstalling the app also removes it.
  • Stop Google backup: More, then Disconnect in the Google backup section. You can also remove Ledger's access at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • Delete the backup in your Drive: on a computer, open Google Drive, then Settings, then Manage apps. Find Ledger, choose Options, then Delete hidden app data.

Children

Ledger is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. Changes that affect how data from Google is used will also be noted in the app's "What's new" screen.

Contact

Questions about privacy can be raised at github.com/Josh-1630/Ledger/issues, or through the support email shown on Ledger's Google sign-in screen.
